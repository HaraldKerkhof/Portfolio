Every piece starts the same way: a single line, repeated and mirrored until it finds its own rhythm. I don't sketch a plan, I let the symmetry guide me, layer by layer, until something ancestral and mechanical meets in the middle.

It's slow work. Some pieces take days of tracing the same curve until it feels right. But that's where the depth comes from not from complexity for its own sake, but from patience.

ALDHARVISIO isn't just a name it's a version of my own, turned inside out. The same goes for the work itself: tribal roots twisted into something new, something that feels like it could belong to the past and the future at once. Every symmetrical form is a way of asking who I am, without needing to say it directly.

I'm obsessed with density the way thin lines, folded and mirrored across a single axis, can pile up into something that feels almost alive.

When you look close, there's chaos: thorns, filigree, mechanical joints, organic curves all fighting for space. But step back, and it settles into balance.

That tension between chaos and order is exactly what I'm chasing in every piece.

People sometimes see this work and think "tattoo flash" and sure, it could live on skin. But for me, it's about building something so precisely mirrored and symmetrical it feels inevitable, and detailed enough that you keep finding new shapes the longer you look. That's the whole point: art that rewards attention.

I never plan where a piece ends, I just start from the center and let it grow outward, mirrored, until it stops asking for more. Some people draw to relax. For me it's the opposite: it's where I focus hardest, where every curve has to earn its place next to the one across from it. This isn't something I do for a project or a deadline. It's the one thing I'd keep making even if no one ever saw it.
