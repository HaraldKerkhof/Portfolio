Euro banknote series

A redesign of the €5, €50 and €100 notes. Each note carries its own European butterfly, drawn by hand in Procreate in my own style and chosen for the colours and shape that suit its note.

Butterflies stand for lightness and freedom, which is exactly what European money should express. Around them sit a famous European monument, the EU flag and the familiar euro details, held together by soft, overlapping geometric shapes.

Chris Schoenherstel

A school project to show what I can do in branding: a full brand identity for Chris, a shoemaker who repairs shoes, bags and belts by hand in his workshop in Ghent. Chris doesn't exist, he and his shop were made up for the assignment.

I drew a handmade logo, built a palette of leather browns with one bold pink accent and carried it through everything a workshop like his would use: price tags, work orders, business cards, the shop front and socials.

Vivienne Westwood jewellery

A photography series of the Vivienne Westwood jewellery I own myself. I shot every piece in the studio with coloured light and paper backdrops, then edited it in my own dreamy style, while keeping the bold spirit of the brand.

The series shows two sides of Westwood: the soft blue of her bridal pieces and the fierce red of her rebellion.

Photography assignment at Arteveldehogeschool. Not commissioned by or affiliated with Vivienne Westwood.

Yakskusha

A city character for Bangkok, based on the Yaksha: the giant guardians that stand watch at the gates of the city's great temples. I kept their crown, their fangs and their strength, and gave him a round body, a big grin and a playful side, so he looks fierce and friendly at the same time.

He is made to stand out for young travellers in Bangkok, so he had to move. In bright temple colours he comes to life in a GIF, an app loading screen, a digital billboard and Instagram stories, posts and posters around the city.

School assignment (Digital Design). Not an official mascot of Bangkok or Thailand.

Every piece starts the same way: a single line, repeated and mirrored until it finds its own rhythm. I don't sketch a plan, I let the symmetry guide me, layer by layer, until something ancestral and mechanical meets in the middle.

It's slow work. Some pieces take days of tracing the same curve until it feels right. But that's where the depth comes from not from complexity for its own sake, but from patience.

ALDHARVISIO isn't just a name it's a version of my own, turned inside out. The same goes for the work itself: tribal roots twisted into something new, something that feels like it could belong to the past and the future at once. Every symmetrical form is a way of asking who I am, without needing to say it directly.

I'm obsessed with density the way thin lines, folded and mirrored across a single axis, can pile up into something that feels almost alive.

When you look close, there's chaos: thorns, filigree, mechanical joints, organic curves all fighting for space. But step back, and it settles into balance.

That tension between chaos and order is exactly what I'm chasing in every piece.

People sometimes see this work and think "tattoo flash" and sure, it could live on skin. But for me, it's about building something so precisely mirrored and symmetrical it feels inevitable, and detailed enough that you keep finding new shapes the longer you look. That's the whole point: art that rewards attention.

I never plan where a piece ends, I just start from the center and let it grow outward, mirrored, until it stops asking for more. Some people draw to relax. For me it's the opposite: it's where I focus hardest, where every curve has to earn its place next to the one across from it. This isn't something I do for a project or a deadline. It's the one thing I'd keep making even if no one ever saw it.
