Brand identity
Shoe and leather repair, Ghent

Chris doesn't exist. This was a school assignment: we got a fictional client and brief, and had to build a complete brand around it to show what we can do in branding.

A complete identity for Chris, a shoemaker who gives leather a second life. Logo, colours and type, but also everything a workshop like his would use: price tags, work orders, business cards, the shop front and socials.

In the brief, Chris didn't want any spectacle. A fresh, contemporary touch was welcome, as long as it stayed honest and true to who he is. So the brand had to feel like his work: handmade, warm and built to last.

Meet Chris (fictional)

Not a shop, a craft

In the story of the brief, Chris has stood for more than twenty years in his workshop in the Sleepstraat in Ghent. He repairs shoes, mends belts and replaces soles, all by hand and with real attention. Whatever people bring in leaves in better shape than it arrived.

People find him through word of mouth, or these days through Facebook and Instagram. New customers don't always know him yet, but once they step inside and see what he does, they trust him straight away.

Vision

A world where people consciously choose repair over replacement, and where craftsmanship and sustainability go hand in hand.

02

Mission

Chris gives leather a second life. With over twenty years of experience he repairs shoes, bags and belts with honesty, precision and care. No fuss.

03

Concept

A craft brand that radiates trust. Strong and contemporary, without losing its roots. Just honest work that speaks for itself.

Who it's for

Two kinds of customers

20–55

The conscious owner

People between 20 and 55 who think about what they buy. They own good shoes or leather goods and would rather have them repaired than throw them away.

18–30

The young rescuer

Young people between 18 and 30 who care about sustainability. They choose repair over fast fashion and throwaway products.

Logo

Round letters, squeezed together

I drew the logo by hand in Illustrator. Heavy, rounded letters that lean into each other and fill every gap, a little like the stitched pieces of a shoe. Bold enough for a shop sign, friendly enough for a price tag.

Next to the full logo there is a short one with just “Chris”, for the small spots: the stamp, the profile picture and the corner of a form. Here both sit in frames of the brand's beige, brown and pink.

Colour

Leather, with one pink stitch

The palette comes from the workshop itself: cream, leather browns and an almost-black. Pink is the one surprise, the accent that makes Chris stand out in the street. Click a colour to copy its code.

Typography

Merriweather Sans

One typeface for everything around the logo. Open and very readable on a small price tag, with enough weight in the Extra Bold to stand next to the heavy letters of the logo.

Visual language

Hands, tools and worn leather

The photos show the craft up close: hands at work, old tools, belts and boots, all in the warm browns of the palette.

Thin line drawings of his tools and a set of ruled lines borrowed from the work order bring a bit of play to the serious leather.

In the street

You see him coming

From the shop window to the hanging sign and the board on the pavement: big pink letters on dark brown and black, so you recognise Chris from across the street.

At the counter

A tag on every repair

The price tag hangs on every shoe, bag or belt that comes in. It doubles as the repair ticket: customer details, the repair, the price and when it can be picked up, signed by the customer. The work order keeps the same information for Chris himself.

The work order

A pad of work orders in two versions, pink and brown, with the short logo at the top. Number, date and a few clear lines, nothing more than Chris needs.

In your hand

Small things, same voice

The business card, the envelope, a stamp for the paperwork and stickers to hand out. Every piece uses the same pink, so even the smallest thing points back to the workshop.

Online

Chris on Instagram and Facebook

A brand like this is often met online first. The posts show Chris at work, the shop and the tags, with the short logo as a round pink profile picture.

Honest work that speaks for itself.
