Yakskusha

A city character for Bangkok
Mascot, key visual, app icon, GIF, loading screen, DOOH, Instagram and posters

School assignment for Digital Design. Yakskusha is not an official mascot of Bangkok or Thailand, and nothing here was commissioned by a real client.

The brief was to give a city its own character. I chose Bangkok, a city where ancient Buddhist temples, wild street life and some of the best food in the world all sit side by side.

Guardian of the temple gates

At the entrance of Bangkok's great temples stand the Yaksha: giant guardians with crowns, fangs and fierce eyes, there to keep evil out. They are everywhere in the city, and that is exactly why I built my character on them. Even the name is a play on Yaksha.

Fierce, but warm

A real Yaksha is meant to scare. Yakskusha is round and strong, with a face that is intimidating and cheerful at the same time. He still protects the city, but he radiates warmth and energy, the way Bangkok itself feels: spiritual and powerful, but also festive.

Made for young travellers

He is not sweet or cute. He has a streetwear attitude, close to tattoo art and Asian street culture, the kind of character you would wear on a hoodie or a cap. He is there to welcome young travellers to the street food, the night markets and the rooftop bars.

Process

From sketch to guardian

It started on paper. I drew the temple guardians as I saw them, with their tall crowns, curled moustaches and wide eyes, until one face felt right: playful, but still a guardian. That face became Yakskusha.

Next I redrew him in Illustrator as clean line art and tried out his poses: standing guard, waving, walking and lounging. As silhouettes they had to work in black alone, so you recognise him from his crown, his ears and his round shape before you even see a colour.

The character

Three moods

In colour he got a green skin like the guardians at the temple gates, a golden crown and a purple shirt. Three poses show what he can be: a guard, a host and a tourist enjoying the city.

Styleguide

Temple colours

The palette comes straight from Bangkok's temples and streets: jade green, deep green and gold from the guardians, warm saffron and orange from the monks' robes and the markets, a calm blue and a deep magenta for the night. Bright, but never random. Click a colour to copy its code.

For the text I chose Newsreader, a classic serif that makes him feel a bit like a travel guide. The name and the tagline are set in a bold italic display face that moves forward, like a tuk-tuk in traffic.

Motion

Brought to life

A city character has to grab attention on a screen, so Yakskusha had to move. In After Effects I animated him for four places where travellers would meet him: on their phone, in an app and on the street.

Key visual & app icon

Bangkok, his way

The key visual puts him in front of the city: lounging with a drink in front of a skyline of temples and towers, with Bangkok written in big shiny letters. I made it in three colourways.

For the app icon only his face remains: crown, ears, beard and that grin, recognisable even at the size of a fingertip.

Out in the city

New in Bangkok

Finally I took him out on the street, in Photoshop mock-ups: a billboard over the rooftops, a light box on a façade and a poster in the metro, each with its own line: "Bangkok, your way to discover" and "Making Bangkok even wilder".

On Instagram he announces himself as new in Bangkok and splits the city into four themes: food, culture, night and shopping. From pad thai and mango sticky rice at Chatuchak to temple hopping at Wat Pho and Wat Arun, sky bars at night and the big markets and malls.
